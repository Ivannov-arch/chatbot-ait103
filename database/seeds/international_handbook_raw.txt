1.1 Overview of Xiamen University
Xiamen University (XMU) is a prestigious public research university located in Xiamen, Fujian, China.
Established in 1921 by the well-known overseas Chinese entrepreneur Tan Kah Kee. XMU is recognized
as one of China's top universities, consistently ranked among the best for academics, research, and global
engagement. It is a Double First-Class University and a member of Project 985 and Project 211 which are
initiatives to promote China's top-tier universities.
1.1.1 Key Features of Xiamen University:
• Academic Excellence: XMU offers a wide range of programs in disciplines such as sciences,
engineering, humanities, business, and medical sciences.
• Research and Innovation: The university is home to numerous research institutes and has strong
collaborations with international institutions.
• Beautiful Campus: The main campus in Xiamen is considered one of the most scenic in China, with
stunning seaside views and traditional Chinese architecture.
• Global Presence: XMU is one of the few Chinese universities with international campuses,
including Xiamen University Malaysia (XMUM), which was established in 2016 as the first
overseas branch of a Chinese university.
1.2 Overview of Malaysia
Malaysia is a vibrant and diverse country in Southeast Asia, known for its rich culture, modern cities,
beautiful landscapes, and strong economy. It is divided into two regions: Peninsular Malaysia and East
Malaysia (on Borneo Island). The capital city, Kuala Lumpur, is a global business and tourism hub, home
to iconic landmarks like the Petronas Twin Towers.
1.2.1 Key Facts About Malaysia
• Official Name: Malaysia
• Capital City: Kuala Lumpur (Administrative capital: Putrajaya)
• Official Language: Malay (Bahasa Malaysia), but English is widely spoken
• Currency: Malaysian Ringgit (MYR)
• Population: About 33 million
• Government: Constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary democracy
• Religion: Islam is the official religion, but Malaysia is multi-religious with Buddhists, Christians,
Hindus, and others.

2.3 Single-Entry Visa (SEV) & eVISA for International Students in Malaysia
International students coming to Malaysia must obtain a Single-Entry Visa (SEV) after receiving their Visa
Approval Letter (VAL). This visa allows them to enter Malaysia and complete their Student Pass
application. Students from eligible countries may also apply for an eVISA (Student), a convenient online
option.
2.3.1 What is a Single-Entry Visa (SEV)?
The SEV is a one-time entry visa required for students from countries that need a visa to enter Malaysia.
It must be obtained before arrival and is issued by the Malaysian Embassy or Consulate in the student’s
home country.
2.3.2 Who Needs an SEV?
• International students from visa-required countries.
• Students from visa-exempt countries (e.g., some ASEAN nations) do not need an SEV but must
still have a VAL.
2.3.3 What is an eVISA (Student)?
Malaysia offers an eVISA (Student) for students from selected countries, allowing them to apply online
instead of visiting an embassy. This electronic visa is valid for a single entry and has the same function as
the SEV.
2.3.4 Who Can Apply for an eVISA?
Students from eligible countries, including:
• China
• India
• Bangladesh
• Sri Lanka
• Myanmar
• Pakistan
• Nepal
📌 Important: Students from other countries must apply for an SEV through the Malaysian Embassy.

3.2 Type of Student Pass/ Student Visa
Malaysia offers student visas to international students pursuing academic programs at recognized
institutions. These visas, managed by Education Malaysia Global Services (EMGS), fall into two primary
categories:
3.2.1 Yearly Student Visa
A yearly student visa is issued for one year and requires annual renewal. This is the standard visa format
for most international students in Malaysia.
Key Features:
a. Valid for one year and must be renewed annually.
b. Renewal depends on academic performance, attendance, and medical screening.
c. Students must apply for renewal at least 3 months before expiry to avoid penalties.
d. Common for foundation, diploma, undergraduate, and postgraduate students.
3.2.2 Full-term Student Visa
A full-term student visa is granted for the entire duration of a student's academic program, typically three
to four years for degree programs.
Key Features:
a. Covers the entire academic program duration.
b. Requires a one-time approval but may require periodic updates.
c. Students must comply with attendance and academic performance requirements to maintain
validity.
d. Reduces the hassle of annual renewals but still requires certain document updates.

3.2.3 How the Type of Student Visa (Yearly vs. Full-Term) Is Determined in Malaysia
The type of student visa an international student receives in Malaysia—yearly or full-term—depends on
various factors, including university policies, immigration requirements, nationality, and other
eligibility criteria set by Education Malaysia Global Services (EMGS) and Immigration Malaysia.
3.2.3.1 Factors That Determine Yearly vs. Full-Term Student Visas
a. Type of Program & Duration
Short-term programs (less than 1 year) → Yearly Visa
• Language courses
• Foundation programs
• Exchange or mobility programs
Long-term programs (1 year or more) → Full-Term Visa (if eligible) or Yearly Visa
• Diploma, Bachelor's, Master's, and PhD programs
• Postgraduate research-based programs are more likely to qualify for full-term visas

3.3 Process for i-Kad and Insurance Renewal for International Students in Malaysia
International students studying in Malaysia are required to renew their i-Kad and medical insurance
annually as part of their student visa renewal process. Below is a detailed guide on the renewal process
for both.
3.3.1 i-Kad Renewal Process
The i-Kad (International Student Card) is an identification card issued by Immigration Malaysia to
international students. It serves as an alternative to carrying a passport for identification within
Malaysia.
3.3.2 Who Needs to Renew the i-Kad?
• All international students studying in Malaysia under a student visa.
• Students who are renewing their student visa.
Processing & Collection
• The university submits the application to EMGS/Immigration Malaysia together with student
visa renewal.
• Once processed, students will be notified to collect their new i-Kad.
• Processing time: 2 to 4 weeks (depending on the university and EMGS processing speed).
3.3.3 Medical Insurance Renewal Process
All international students in Malaysia must have valid medical insurance throughout their studies.
Insurance renewal is a mandatory requirement for student visa renewal.
Who Needs to Renew Insurance?
• All students with yearly student visas.
• Students with a full-term visa but yearly insurance policies.
Processing & Collection
• The university submits the application to EMGS/Immigration Malaysia together with student
visa renewal.
• Once processed, students will be able to view new policy via the insurance App
• Processing time: 2 to 4 weeks (depending on the university and EMGS processing speed).

3.4 International Student Visa Renewal Process
International students must renew their Student Pass (Visa) before its expiration to maintain legal status
in Malaysia. The renewal process should begin 3 months before expiry.
3.4.1 Eligibility for Renewal
To be eligible for student visa renewal, you must:
• Maintain good academic performance and attendance (least 80% attendance and a CGPA of
2.0 or above).
• Have no disciplinary issues or legal violations in Malaysia.
• Ensure your passport is valid for at least 12 months from the renewal date.
Step 1: Required Documents for Renewal
• Valid passport (with at least 12 months validity)
• Current Student Pass
Step 2: Submission of Documents
• Submit all documents to the International Student Affairs Office (ISAO).
• The university will submit the application to Education Malaysia Global Services (EMGS) for
processing.
Step 3: Approval and Passport Submission
• Once the renewal is approved, students must submit their passport to the International Student
Affairs Office for visa endorsement.
• Processing time: 4 to 6 weeks.
• International students must submit physical passport to ISAO 1 month before expiry date if the
EMGS approval is still pending.
Step 4: Collection of Renewed Student Pass
• After processing, students can collect their passport with the renewed Student Pass from the
International Student Affairs Office.
• Always check the validity of the renewed visa to ensure compliance.
3.4.2 Important Notes
• Always carry a copy of your passport and student pass while in Malaysia.
• Follow university guidelines on visa renewal and compliance.
• Late renewal may result in fines or deportation, so ensure timely submission.
• If facing difficulties, visit the International Student Office for assistance.

3.5 Transfer Program as an International Student in Malaysia
International students studying in Malaysia may wish to change their study program due to academic
interests, career goals, or other reasons. However, changing programs involves specific procedures as
regulated by Education Malaysia Global Services (EMGS) and Immigration Malaysia.
3.5.1 Key Considerations Before Changing Programs
Before applying for a program change, students must consider:
i. Eligibility: Some universities and course es have specific entry requirements.
ii. Visa Implications: A change in program may require a new student visa application or an update
to the existing one.
iii. Credit Transfer: If moving to a related field, some credits may be transferable.
iv. Approval from the University & EMGS: Not all program changes are automatically approved.
3.5.2 Steps to Change a Study Program in Malaysia
A. Changing Programs Within the Same University
If the student is switching to another program within the same university, the process is simpler.
1. Consult the University
o Discuss the program change with the university's international student office and
academic advisor.
o Confirm eligibility and entry requirements for the new program.
2. Obtain University Approval
o Submit a formal request to change programs.
o The university will issue a new offer letter for the chosen program.
3. Apply for new program visa
o Current student visa must be cancelled and a new visa application will be initiated.
o International student who transfers programme within 12 months can proceed for
transfer without returning to home country.
o International students exceeding 12 months must return to home country during the
process of new application.
4. Submit Updated Documents to ISAO
o New offer letter from the university.
o Latest academic transcripts and attendance records.
o Passport copy and current student visa details.

3.6 International Student Deferment Process
International students at Xiamen University Malaysia may apply for deferment due to medical, financial,
personal, or other valid reasons. Approval is subject to university regulations and visa requirements.
3.6.1 Eligibility Criteria
• Students cannot defer during their first semester.
• Deferment is only allowed once during the entire programme duration.
• Valid supporting documents (e.g., medical reports, financial statements) must be provided.
• The deferment period must comply with the university’s maximum allowable duration.
3.6.2 Visa and Immigration Considerations
• International students must consult the International Student Affairs Office (ISAO) regarding their
student visa status.
• Students planning to leave Malaysia must inform ISAO to avoid visa complications.
• If the visa expires during the deferment period, students may need to apply for a new visa before
returning.
3.6.3 Returning to Studies
• Students must notify the university at least one month before their intended return.
• Resumption procedures may include academic and visa clearance.

4.0 Rules and Regulations for International Students in Malaysia
International students in Malaysia must comply with Malaysian laws, university regulations, and student
visa requirements set by Education Malaysia Global Services (EMGS) and the Immigration Department of
Malaysia. Any involvement in illegal activities can result in visa cancellation, deportation, legal action, and
permanent blacklisting from entering Malaysia in the future.
4.1 Immigration and Visa Regulations
a. Student Visa and Pass
• International students must hold a valid student visa issued by the Malaysian Immigration
Department.
• Students must renew their visa before expiry to avoid overstaying penalties.
• A student visa is strictly for study purposes and cannot be used for employment unless permitted
by immigration.
• Engaging in illegal activities, unauthorized employment, or falsifying documents will lead to
immediate deportation.
b. Overstaying & Visa Violations
• Overstaying is a serious offense and can result in heavy fines, detention, deportation, and
blacklisting.
• Students who withdraw, complete, or transfer their studies must cancel their visa and leave
Malaysia immediately.
